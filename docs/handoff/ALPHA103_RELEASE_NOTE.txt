alpha103: the deep low-end research, three speed experiments, and a test fix. (1) RESEARCH: research/LOW_END_PLAYBOOK.md compares what popular browsers do for speed on weak hardware with what Kestrel does today, using your own tablet numbers: four ARM cores, Mali-G52 graphics, 1954 MB of memory, Android 10 Go with a 32-bit only system, and no heat slowdown in any of 35 uploads. Almost everything popular browsers do, Kestrel already does. What is left and measured: the engine's main thread inside the app process is the busiest competitor for your four cores (it carries the ad blocker; median 34 percent of one core when busy, in 121 of 175 busy moments), then the disk cache thread, the engine's tracking list updater (rare but up to 96 percent of a core) and the app's own memory clean-up; those are the next tasks. Android 10 Go specifics I found: its memory warnings do not follow free memory (filtered since alpha101), apps in the background die first (hence the start screen after 4 hours, alpha100), this device has no performance hint or sustained performance mode to call, and a 64-bit engine cannot run on a 32-bit only system. (2) THREE EXPERIMENTS in Settings, Network lab, all OFF: Cheaper gradients (no dithering), Compile scripts on first load, and Parallel script clean-up. I claim no gain: each is a setting your engine has that popular browser notes point at, and only your tablet can judge it. Each takes effect at the next start of Kestrel and every upload records it, so my report compares page times with and without. Turn them on one at a time for a day. (3) The download test that failed once on the build computer, because a 30 second wait ran out on its two slow cores, now waits longer, and failing tests print their message; this changes nothing in the app. Not run on the tablet: the three switches are tested in unit tests and the engine setting names are checked against your engine.
