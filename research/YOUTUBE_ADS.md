# YouTube ad blocking and skipping - notes (RX3-Y1..Y5, RX3-G7, RX4)

Rows: RX3-Y1..Y5 and RX3-G7 (research/REVIEW-EXTERNAL-3.md part Y), RX4 (the owner's screenshot of 2026-11-14). Newest finding first. Everything here is ASCII; numbers carry device, build and date.

## 1. YouTube IS reachable from the cloud sandbox (2026-11-14; corrects REVIEW-EXTERNAL-3 section 12 and Y3)
The review said real YouTube was never reached from the sandbox. It can be: Playwright Chromium started with `proxy: { server: process.env.HTTPS_PROXY }` and nothing else (the agent proxy's CA is already in the browser trust store).
Probe: `node tools/sitelab/yt_probe.js <video id>` prints the page's own `ytInitialPlayerResponse.playabilityStatus`, the count of `adPlacements` and the visible text for three user agents (mobile site with the tablet's Firefox-Android UA, mobile site with a Chrome-Android UA, desktop site).
Limits: Chromium is not Gecko. It shows what YouTube's SERVERS answer and which data and selectors exist, not how the tablet's engine behaves. A first visit without cookies sometimes gets a 302 (probably the consent or bot page): a live check must follow it or set the consent cookie (open item for RX3-Y3).

## 3. RX3-Y1 - the lists' own YouTube answer (alpha41)
Data (the lists the app downloads, `filters.min.txt` + `quick-fixes.min.txt`, fetched 2026-11-14; the lines are kept as `core/src/test/resources/youtube-scriptlets.txt`): `www.youtube.com` has 33 scriptlet rules (before this change 9 were skipped: one `rpnt` of 3,468 characters, six `trusted-edit-inbound-object`, two `trusted-prevent-dom-bypass`), `m.youtube.com` (the tablet's default site) has 11 (1 skipped: `json-edit-fetch-response`, `..itemSectionRenderer.contents.*[?.adSlotRenderer]` on requests containing `/get_watch`: it cuts the ad slots out of the mobile feed). All 31 distinct lines are accepted now when the list is trusted.
Built:
- Caps for trusted lists (`MAX_ARG_TRUSTED` 4096, `MAX_TOTAL_TRUSTED` 8192; per host `MAX_CALLS` 64, `MAX_PAYLOAD` 32,000; the library accepts up to 40,000 characters and 70 calls per send). A list that is not trusted keeps 500 / 1500.
- New names in `ScriptletRules`: `json-edit`, `json-edit-fetch-response`, `json-edit-xhr-response`, `trusted-json-edit`, `trusted-json-edit-fetch-response`, `trusted-json-edit-xhr-response`, `trusted-edit-inbound-object` (three arguments), `trusted-prevent-dom-bypass` (two).
- The JSON edit engine in `scriptlets-main.js` (ES5, bounded: 120,000 nodes, depth 60, never throws into the page; written from the behaviour the public uBO wiki documents and from the 88 real list lines that use it): steps `..name`, `.name`, `.*`, `./regex/`, `[?test]`; tests `.a.b`, `==`, `*=`, `^=`, `$=`, `=/re/`; actions delete (array items are cut out, no holes), `=<json>`, `+={}`, `=repl({"regex","replacement"})`; `$` and `v2:` prefixes; named arguments `propsToMatch` and `matches` and the positional request needle. Only the trusted names may set, merge or replace. A fetch/XHR rule without a request needle is refused (it would parse every answer of the page). One `JSON.parse` hook serves prune and edit rules, with a cheap text check first.
- `trusted-edit-inbound-object` wraps a function (JSON.stringify) and edits the argument the page hands in, in place, just before the call. `trusted-prevent-dom-bypass` makes a frame inserted with `appendChild` carry OUR `fetch` / `Request`.
- Accounting: `BuildReport.unsupportedNames` and `scriptletSummary()`; the updater stores the one line next to the lists (`files/filters/scriptlets.txt`) and Diagnostics shows it plus "YouTube scriptlet calls loaded: www N, m N".
Proof: `ScriptletRulesTest` (28 + 7 tests incl. every real YouTube line accepted from a trusted list and the same lines from an untrusted one), `web-tests/jsonedit.test.js` (19 tests; the real mobile line on a synthetic feed, the real inbound lines on synthetic request bodies, the guard against a fresh frame's own fetch).
NOT proven: that any of this works on real YouTube inside Gecko. Only the mechanics were tested, with synthetic payloads. The desktop site's enforcement pipeline (`rpnt` that tags the client with markers and reloads on a stalled player, plus the inbound edits that react to the markers) is a re-implementation of what the lists expect: if desktop-mode YouTube misbehaves after alpha41, switching "Fix ad and anti-adblock scripts" off (Settings) or Shields off for youtube.com is the undo. The tablet's default mobile site only gets the feed edit and the rules that were already working.

## 4. RX3-Y2 - the built-in ad skipper (alpha41)
Why: the lists' own YouTube answer (section 3) changes every few hours and our daily refresh lags; a client-side skipper works in both site modes and is a safety net that does not depend on list data.
Markup checked LIVE on 2026-11-14 (headless Chromium, mobile and desktop user agent, an ad-free sample video; scratch scripts, not kept): `#movie_player` exists and IS the `.html5-video-player` element on the mobile site (`ytp-mweb-player`) and on the desktop site; its video is `video.video-stream.html5-main-video` with a `blob:` (MSE) source; none of the ad selectors (`.ytp-skip-ad-button`, `.ytp-ad-skip-button`, `.ytp-ad-skip-button-modern`, `.ytp-ad-skip-button-container button`, `.ytp-ad-overlay-close-button`, `.video-ads`, `.ytp-ad-module`, `ytd-enforcement-message-view-model`, `tp-yt-paper-dialog`) is in the DOM while no ad runs (`.video-ads` and `.ytp-ad-module` are created with the first ad). VERIFIED: the player and the video elements. NOT VERIFIED: the ad state class names `ad-showing` / `ad-interrupting`, the skip-button selectors, the overlay button and the enforcement dialog: the sandbox is served NO ad as a signed-out visitor (six popular videos tried on both sites: none had ad placements, several asked to sign in), so no real ad could be observed. They are the long-standing public names. The owner's Diagnostics line "YouTube ads" is the proof on the real site.
What it does (`geckoapp/src/main/assets/kestrel-shields/youtube.js`, isolated world, manifest `content_scripts` for `*://*.youtube.com/*`, `*://*.youtube-nocookie.com/*`, `*://*.youtubekids.com/*`, all frames, document_end):
- Nothing runs until an ad class is on the player: the player is found once (and again at +1 s, +3 s, +8 s and after the site's `yt-navigate-finish`), only its `class` attribute is observed. No polling without an ad.
- While an ad shows (a 250 ms loop, 15 s at most): mute, `playbackRate = 16`, an ad stream shorter than 3 minutes is moved to its last 0.1 s, the first visible skip button is pressed once, overlay ads are closed. The film is never moved: the jump needs the video's length to differ from the film's (remembered while no ad shows), because the ad class can come a moment before the ad stream does. When the class goes the sound and the speed are put back unless somebody else already did.
- Overlay (banner) ads have no ad class: the `.video-ads` element is watched for new children (small, not the whole page).
- The "ad blockers are not allowed" dialog is looked for at 1.5 s, 4 s and 9 s after the page and after each navigation (the element `ytd-enforcement-message-view-model`, or a paper dialog with "ad blocker" in its text); it and its backdrops are removed, scrolling unlocked, the film started.
- Five ads in a row still showing after 15 s switch it off for the page. Counters (numbers only, differences, one report per 3 s at most) go to the app: Diagnostics "YouTube ads: ..." and Health `ytads report`. Private tabs are dropped.
- Setting: "Skip ads inside video players (YouTube and others)", default ON, with the warning that YouTube's terms forbid it and it may warn or limit playback (more likely when signed in). The per-page answer to the content scripts' `guard` question carries `skip` (flag bit 8 of the page config: Shields up for the site AND the Settings row); nothing is done unless it is exactly true.
Proof: `web-tests/youtube.test.js` (14 tests against a mock player built on the live markup: an ad of 12 s ends in about two seconds and the sound and the speed come back; ad-interrupting; nothing touched without an ad class; a skip button is pressed; an overlay is closed; the dialog, its backdrop and the scroll lock are removed and the film started; what the owner had is kept; the film itself is never thrown to its end; give-up after repeated stuck ads; the Settings row off; navigation replaces the player; numbers-only counters; the manifest scope), `AdSkipStatsTest` (7).
Not run: a real ad (none available), the tablet, Gecko. YouTube may answer with warnings, a stalled player or a sign-in demand; it is an arms race and the owner's report decides what is next. G7 (the same idea for other players) is not built yet.

## 6. RX3-G7 - the ad skipper for video players other than YouTube (alpha42)
Row: RX3-G7 (owner: "yes to all four", part (d) skip ads inside video players by default). File: `geckoapp/src/main/assets/kestrel-shields/players.js` (isolated world, every http(s) page and frame, `document_start`, `exclude_matches` the three YouTube host families that `youtube.js` owns). Tests: `web-tests/players.test.js` (13, real headless Chromium, mock players), `web-tests/background.test.js` (manifest + report relay), core `AdSkipStatsTest`.

### 6.1 The class names, READ in the players' own public source (2026-11-14), not remembered
The review listed `jw-flag-ads`, `vjs-ad-playing`, `vjs-ad-loading`, `ima-ad-container`, `plyr--ad`. The sources (jsdelivr / the JW CDN, kept outside the repo) say:
| Player (version read) | What its source does while an ad plays | Used by players.js |
| :- | :- | :- |
| Video.js + videojs-contrib-ads 7.5.2 | `player.addClass('vjs-ad-playing')` in `startLinearAdMode`, removed when the ad ends; `vjs-ad-loading` while it WAITS for the ad (no ad stream yet); `vjs-ad-content-resuming` while the film comes back | `vjs-ad-playing` = an ad. The other two are waiting states: ignored (nothing to skip, and muting the film then would be wrong) |
| JW Player 8.33.1 | adds `jw-flag-ads` to its root when the instream (ad) is set up, removes `jw-flag-ads` and `jw-flag-ads-hide-controls` when it ends; the skip button is a `div.jw-skip` that gets `jw-skippable` once the wait is over; its click is wired through the player's own UI helper | `jw-flag-ads` = an ad; press `.jw-skip.jw-skippable` (pointerdown, mousedown, pointerup, mouseup, click: the helper may listen to the up event) |
| Google IMA through videojs-ima 2.3.0 | the ad container is `div.ima-ad-container` (absolute, z-index 1111); the SDK's own ad UI (skip, learn more) is Google's and mostly sits in cross-origin frames | a `<video>` INSIDE `ima-ad-container` is the ad. Its skip button cannot be reached |
| Plyr 3.7.8 | NO state class on the root: the ads plugin gives IMA a `div.plyr__ads` container (`classNames.ads`). `plyr--ad` from the review does not exist | a `<video>` inside `plyr__ads` is the ad |
IMA's own sdkloader file holds no UI class names (the UI is loaded later in frames), so `videoAdUi*` names from memory were NOT used.

### 6.2 What it does, and what it refuses to do
- No work until a `<video>` fires `play`; then only the `class` attribute of its ancestors (8 levels, never body) is watched, one observer, no polling. The app (the `guard` question) is asked only the first time an ad word shows: a page without ad words never causes a message. Answer must carry `skip === true` (Settings row on AND Shields up for the site).
- While an ad word shows AND the element holds a stream that is NOT the film (a hosted ad video, or a finite stream whose length differs from the film's remembered length by more than 0.5 s; a live stream counts as the film): mute, 16x, move an ad stream shorter than 3 minutes to its last 0.1 s after 300 ms, press the skip button. Loop 250 ms, 15 s at most. When the stream is still the film nothing is touched (the class can arrive before the ad stream). Sound and speed are put back at the end unless the page changed them.
- Five ads in a row still showing after 15 s switch it off for that frame. Reports are numbers plus a few words with `w:'p'`; Diagnostics has its own line "Other players' ads: ...".
- A known soft spot of the YouTube skipper (youtube.js) that this script does NOT share: youtube.js applies mute and 16x as soon as the ad class shows, even if the element still holds the film. YouTube sets the class and loads the ad stream together and the film is paused then, so it is judged harmless; if Diagnostics ever shows "gave up" on YouTube, apply the same "not the film" test there (row RX3-Y4 area).

### 6.3 What is NOT known
A real ad from these players was never seen (the sandbox gets none): the mocks only prove the script does what this section says when the class names above appear. Whether Gecko 160 lets a content script set `playbackRate` / `muted` / `currentTime` on a page's video and dispatch mouse events to the player's own listeners is shown on the emulator lab (see section 6.4 when it exists) and otherwise by the owner's Diagnostics line on the tablet.

### 6.4 Emulator proof (2026-11-14; Android 10 x86_64 emulator, GeckoView 160 debug build, software rendering: NOT the tablet, timings only show the order of magnitude)
Lab: `tools/sitelab/guardlab.py` page `/playerlab` (five mock players on one page, every ad a REAL 12 s silent media element, driven by the page itself) + `tools/emu/playerlab_emu.py` (cold start from a link, prints what the page measured and the app's Health lines).
| Scenario | Without the skipper the ad takes | Measured in the real engine | Verdict |
| :- | :- | :- | :- |
| ordinary video, no ad words | - | never at 16x in 163 samples, still playing | untouched |
| JW-style root with `jw-flag-ads` (first ever ad of the page, 2.8 s to the first fast sample = the first question to the app on a slow emulator) | 12000 ms | warm-up ad 3423 ms; the next JW ad 886 ms (first fast sample after 485 ms) | fast-forwarded |
| Video.js-style: `vjs-ad-loading` only, then `vjs-ad-playing` | 12000 ms | waiting state: 0 of 9 samples at 16x; the ad 780 ms | waiting ignored, ad skipped |
| Google-IMA-style: ad video inside `.ima-ad-container`, the film video beside it | 12000 ms | ad 1137 ms; the film video: 0 of 15 samples at 16x, still playing | only the ad touched |
| JW-style `.jw-skip` that becomes `.jw-skippable` after 1.5 s | never ends (the lab loops it) | pressed by the script 4012 ms after the ad began, never before it was skippable; the page's listeners got pointerdown, mousedown, pointerup, mouseup, click | pressed once |
App side (Health, same run): `ad skippers: asked 1, allowed 1, reports 6` in the extension line, `ads report-in ... kept`, `playerads report ads=2 skipped=1 ... jumps=1`: the numbers reach the app and the Diagnostics line "Other players' ads". The `gaveUp=1 an ad was still showing after 15 s` in the same run is the lab's last scenario, which leaves its ad class on after the click (fixed in the lab since).
What this proves: in Gecko 160 a content script sees a video's `play`, watches the ancestors' class, sets `playbackRate`, `muted` and `currentTime` on a page's video, and its dispatched mouse events reach the page's own listeners. It does NOT prove what a real JW / Video.js / IMA ad does (none was available), nor the tablet's speed.
### 6.5 Two defects the emulator found before release (both fixed, both with a test)
1. The first version followed at most 4 videos per page; in the lab the ad video was the 5th to play and was ignored (ad took 13102 ms). Now 12, and a video that is paused or ended is forgotten first (`web-tests/players.test.js` "a page with many videos" fails with the old cap).
2. The report relay named the page only from the tab map, which does not know the FIRST page after a cold start (or a restored tab nobody navigated); the app then dropped every report as "private or unknown page". This also hid the YouTube skipper's numbers in alpha41. The relay now falls back to the top frame's own address (`background.test.js` RX3-G7). Frames of such a tab still name no page and are dropped (the safe side).
