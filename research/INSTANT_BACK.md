# INSTANT_BACK: "other browsers can immediately go back to the last page" (owner, 2026-11-23, PERF13)
Owner, verbatim: "Improve a page's first load on open and a page reload when i press the back button. Figure out what popular browsers do for this. Right now it pauses with a box that says loading, but other browsers can immediately go back to the last page".
Earlier work this builds on (do not redo): research/BACK_SCROLL.md (RX3-BK1..BK5, BK10 Instant Back, the Back lab), research/COLD_START.md (the last-look picture at the next start), research/SPEED3.md section 2 (the engine's back/forward cache blockers).

## 1. What the popular browsers do (sources read 2026-11-23: web.dev / developer.chrome.com bfcache pages, Mozilla bugs on session history and low memory, WebKit and Chromium history-navigation threads)
| Technique | Chrome (Android) | Safari / iOS | Firefox / Fenix | Kestrel G today |
| :- | :- | :- | :- | :- |
| **Back/forward cache** (bfcache): the whole page, with its script state, frozen in memory and thawed on Back; this is the "instant" you see | yes (Android since 96); unload handlers do not block on mobile; `Cache-Control: no-store` pages are allowed since Chrome ~116 until a cookie or an authorization change (3 min instead of 10) | yes, for years | yes, in the engine; on mobile Firefox does not cache pages with unload handlers (this engine's `allow_unload_listeners` is already true) | yes (engine's own), `max_total_viewers = 6`, one hour; `no-store` rewritten to `no-cache` by the extension (Instant Back, BK10); proven kept on the real engine in the lab (run 44873006097) |
| What makes a page ineligible | in-flight requests of some kinds, pages still loading, open connections / locks, a few APIs | similar | pending requests (REQUEST), still loading (PAGE_LOADING), MSE / EME video, camera / mic, an opener | the same engine rules; ordinary busy pages (ad and analytics requests open, a page left before its load ends, a video player, an opener) are exactly what it refuses |
| **Memory pressure** | the cache is evicted first | evicted | **every cached page is evicted** on a memory-pressure event (Mozilla session-history bugs) | the engine gets memory pressure for Android trim levels 15 (RUNNING_CRITICAL), 40 to 79 ("ongoing"), 80 and up, and onLowMemory (read from the engine's own class `MemoryController`); levels 5, 10 and 20 (the app went to the background) do nothing; the tablet logs trims of 15 and 80 every hour or so |
| **History navigations use the HTTP cache without asking the server** (no revalidation) | yes | yes | yes | engine behaviour; the Back lab measures it as `kind=cache` (no network) |
| **Scroll position restored with the page** | yes, and it waits for the page to be tall enough | yes | yes, but gives up early on pages that grow | the engine restores; Kestrel's content script pulls the place from the app and scrolls when the page is tall enough |
| **Keep showing the old page until the new one paints** ("paint holding"; a screenshot of the target during Android 14+ predictive back) | yes | partly | partly | yes: the last-look and Back pictures stay until the page is painted at its place |
| **First load**: connect early (preconnect on typing, on touch-down, to the top suggestion), DNS prefetch, HTTP cache, HTTP/3, speculative prefetch / prerender of the likely next page, a pre-started renderer process, session restore that shows the old page picture first | all of them | most | most | already: warm connections (typed suggestion, results, touch), look-ahead DNS, speculation rules (the owner has them ON), the HTTP/3 switch, `engine warm` at start, the last-look picture at the next start. Not possible here: prerendering a whole page (2 GB, CPU-bound pages) |

Conclusion: the only thing that gives "immediately" is a **bfcache hit**. Everything else (cache, picture) shortens the wait but does not remove it.

## 2. What the owner's tablet actually shows (all 22 uploads read; Health `back press` lines, normal tabs only)
- 29 Back presses in 3 days. 22 were `kind=other`: Back inside a single-page app (a video site's mobile pages, a chat app, a search page): no page load happens, the site's own script handles it (that site shows its own loading bar); nothing a browser can do, Chrome has the same.
- 7 presses led to a real page load (two shopping pages, a video site's pages). **0 were kept by the engine's cache.** 5 were reloaded from the HTTP cache (`kind=cache`, no network) and 2 from the network. Each took 2.5 to 5.7 s from the address change to the page's own `pageshow` (median 3.4 s), 1.3 to 2.7 s to the first paint (median 1.6 s), and **the scroll place was missed in 4 of 6 measured presses** (once: wanted 5858 px, got 991 px).
- So on this tablet Back is NOT instant for ordinary pages although the lab proves the engine can keep them: pages left on the hotspot are mostly still loading, have requests open, or hold a video player, and the engine refuses them. The app's own guess (`notKeptWhy`, `back-arm ... notKept=no`) said "will be kept" for those presses, so the guess misses the real reason.
- The "box that says loading" was the Back picture (the saved picture of the target, a thin line at the top after 1 s and the note "Still loading <site>" after 4 s) in front of a page that is being parsed again; alpha96 drops the note box and shows the line after 0.6 s; at the start of the app the same overlay says "Opening <site>" until the real page paints (`start paint cold` 7 to 28 s: a heavy chat page makes about 1200 requests and is about 95 % late).

## 3. What alpha96 changes (PERF13c, SHIPPED when green, NOT confirmed on the tablet)
1. **The reason is measured, not guessed.** content.js sends the engine's own verdict at the moment a page is left (`pagehide.persisted`, and whether the page had finished loading); the Back lab joins it to the next press to that page: `back press ... to=<host> left=kept | refused-loaded | refused-loading | ?`. Counters `back.left.kept` / `back.left.refused`. The digest has a BACK section that says which of the three cases dominates.
2. **The note box is gone**: the Back picture no longer says "Still loading <site>" after 4 s (the thin loading line along the top edge stays, now after 0.6 s instead of 1 s): other browsers show the page, not a notice.
3. **The place is no longer given up after 3 s**: 8 looks in 9 s (was 5 in 3 s) for pages that grow slowly; each look acts only when the page is tall enough and never after a touch (web-tests/back.test.js: a page that becomes tall after 5.2 s).
4. No change to the engine's cache prefs this release: the candidates are below with the evidence each needs.

## 4. Not done, ranked by expected gain (decide from the `left=` lines of the next upload)
1. If pages are `refused-loading` (left before the load ends, likely on this link): cancel a page's still-open low-value requests (ads, analytics, images below the fold) when the person taps a link, so the engine may keep the page; risk: broken images when the cached page is restored. Lab pages for pending fetch / XHR / WebSocket are still missing (BACK_SCROLL.md section 3) and must exist before any experiment.
2. If pages are `refused-loaded`: find the blocker per page kind in the lab (unload / beforeunload, MSE, `window.opener`, open fetch streams): `docshell.shistory.bfcache.require_no_opener=false` is the one pref with a known trade-off (a cached page keeps a link to its opener).
3. A trim of 15 or 80 reaches the engine and flushes all cached pages; Chrome and Safari do the same under real pressure. Nothing to do except keep the app's own memory low.
4. Back pictures on disk (today 8 pictures in memory, all dropped at any trim of 15 or more): a Back after a trim shows the plain "Opening" cover. A small disk store like the last-look files (never for a private tab) would keep them; worth doing if the digest shows `back-arm ... picture=false` often (1 of 15 in the last upload).
5. First open: a lazy restore of the last tab (show the picture, load the page only when the person touches it or after the app has been idle on it) would save 8 to 28 s of CPU on heavy pages; it changes behaviour (the page is not live until touched): owner decision.

## 5. alpha96 uploads (2026-11-23, after the owner installed it): the engine's own verdicts
Two real page Backs on the tablet with the new `left=` field (a news article page and a search results page): `kind=network`, `left=refused-loaded`, `notKept=loading` (the app's own state said the page was still loading when it was left), `show` 7.1 s and 16.4 s, first paint 4.2 s and 2.0 s, place missed in both.
Reading: the page had FINISHED loading for the person and for the page's own script (`document.readyState` was complete at `pagehide`) but the engine's own load state was still "loading" (late scripts, frames and styles of ad and tracking hosts on a 5 Mbit/s hotspot), and an engine page that is still loading is never put into the back/forward cache. That is the owner's "I was already on the page and it was already loaded": true for the person, not for the engine. The lab test that a hanging IMAGE does not block the cache (BACK_SCROLL.md section 2, round "loading") fits: images are background loads, scripts, styles and frames are not.
Change (alpha97): when the person taps an ordinary link (left click, same tab, no modifier, the page did not handle the click itself, a real new page, no media playing) the content script calls `window.stop()` from a click listener that runs AFTER the page's own handlers and BEFORE the link's navigation starts (the link default action is queued after the event has been dispatched). That cancels the unfinished loads of the page that is being left, so the engine can keep it. The cost: pictures that were still loading are not completed when the cached page is restored (below-the-fold pictures of an ad-heavy page); a restored page whose script waited for such a load keeps waiting. Switch: Settings > Instant Back (default ON) now covers this too. `back press ... left=` adds `+stop` when the stop was used so the next upload shows whether it made the pages `kept`.
