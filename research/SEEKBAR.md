# YS1: a tap on a video player's bar must jump the video (owner message 42, 2026-11-15)
ASCII only. Newest section last. Written the moment the facts existed.

## 1. The owner's words and what they mean
"I'm on YouTube and the videos have a red seek bar. The red line is the portion of the video already watched. The red dot is the current part ... The rest of the line is a light color to the right side of the red dot for unwatched video. When I press anywhere on this bar on a regular browser or in YouTube app, it will jump the video to the place I pressed on the bar ... But in Kestrel, it doesn't jump the video at all, it just makes a strange light colored line up to the point I pressed. I want the seek bar for videos inside the browser to act like other browsers and video player apps -- if I press a spot on the video player bar, it should jump the video to that part of the bar. It should start buffering video from that point forward and not buffer anything or waste any internet on any part of the video prior to the part I am currently watching."
- "a strange light colored line up to the point I pressed" = the player's HOVER line (YouTube's `.ytp-hover-progress`, a light line from the start of the bar to the pointer). So the page DID see the touch as a pointer over the bar, and did not jump.
- Two asks: (1) the tap must jump; (2) after the jump the data must be fetched from the new place on, nothing before it.

## 2. What could and could not be found (honest)
- The sandbox cannot load YouTube's watch page or embed (the agent proxy gets the bot-check page, `solveSimpleChallenge`; a Firefox user agent on Chromium is challenged too; the emulator's HTTPS to real sites rendered blank). tools/sitelab/yt_seek.js exists for the day a real page can be had.
  So the cause on the owner's tablet is NOT known. YouTube's bar code was not read either (not available).
- Ruled out by reading our own code: the only places that write a video's `currentTime` are the ad skippers (players.js:166, youtube.js:108), and only while an ad class is present; nothing reverts a seek; no script stops, cancels or replays touch / pointer / mouse events on a player (only `contextmenu` on videos in content.js and anchors in guard-main.js). The engine prefs (gecko-prefs.txt) hold no touch / pointer / mouse / hover prefs. KestrelGeckoView forwards every touch.
- Not ruled out: Gecko's fingerprinting protection (`setFingerprintingProtection(true)` in GeckoEngine.kt) changes what a page sees of a touch (pointer type, id) - see section 6 for what the emulator showed; YouTube's own mobile UI may need `touchstart` / `touchend` of a kind Gecko delivers differently; a slow press (over 300 ms) is not a "tap" to the engine's gesture detector.

## 3. Decision: a safety net that works whatever the cause (seekbar.js)
A content script (isolated world, every http(s) page and frame, document start, passive listeners only) that watches taps and slides on a player's bar and moves the video ITSELF when the page did not:
1. At `pointerdown` (only when the page has a `<video>`; otherwise nothing happens at all): the stack of elements under the finger (`elementsFromPoint`, 14 deep) is searched for a bar: a range input, `role=slider`, or a name with progress / seek / scrub / timeline / slider / playhead; rejected when the name says volume, speed, quality, caption, ad, lesson ...; shape: at least 100 CSS px wide, at most 72 high, at least 5 times wider than high; the WIDEST such element wins (YouTube nests several). It must lie over or right beside (32 px) a `<video>` of at least 120x68 and be at least half as wide as it; it and its parents must be visible (opacity product at least 0.5: the first tap on hidden controls only brings them up); no button / link / field may lie over it at the finger.
2. At `pointerup` it is a TAP (under 900 ms, under 14 px) or a SLIDE along the bar (mostly horizontal, under 8 s). A long press or a vertical swipe is not ours (the mini panel's long press stays intact).
3. 450 ms later: if any media element over that picture fired `seeking` since the finger went down (our own seeks are not counted), the PAGE did it: nothing more is done (counted as "page jumped by itself"). Otherwise the app is asked (`guard` answer carries `seek`; flag 16; the Settings row "Tap the video bar to jump", default on, nothing is done unless it is exactly true) and `video.currentTime` is set to (finger x - bar left) / bar width * length (mirrored for a right-to-left bar), clamped to `seekable`.
4. Left alone, with the reason in the report: live stream (length Infinity), length unknown / not ready, an ad plays (`ad-showing`, `ad-interrupting`, `jw-flag-ads`, `vjs-ad-playing`), the bar's own `aria-valuemax` is neither the film's length (seconds or milliseconds) nor 1 / 100, the Settings switch is off, an element vanished.
- Never: preventDefault, stopPropagation, synthesized events, `click()`, `fastSeek`, `play()` (a paused video stays paused, a playing one keeps playing). Pinned by OwnerRulesTest.
- Cost: one `getElementsByTagName('video')` per touch on a page without video; one hit test on a page with one. The report (k:'sbk': t taps, s page jumped, f Kestrel jumped, o left alone, r reason; never an address) goes out at most every 3 s and only when something was counted; the app drops private tabs.
- What it can NOT do: a player that draws its bar on a canvas or inside a closed shadow root; a bar with no name and no slider role; a bar that is part of a native `<video controls>` (the engine's own bar works by itself).

## 4. Buffering "from that point forward"
Not our code: after `currentTime` changes, the engine / the page's player asks for what it needs.
- A progressive file (MP4 / WebM / WAV on a plain server): the engine's media cache turns the new time into a byte offset and sends a NEW range request starting there; the part before it is not read again. `gecko-prefs.txt` limits the read-ahead (`media.cache_readahead_limit` 60 s, resume threshold 30 s, cache 64 MB) so that at most about a minute past the playhead is fetched. The lab page /seekbar-lab proves it with a real 300 s file and logs every Range header (section 6).
- A streaming player (YouTube, HLS, DASH through MSE): the page's own player asks for the segments of the new place (YouTube does not fetch what lies before the playhead after a jump); it keeps a short back buffer of what is already downloaded.
- So nothing of "waste" is added by the jump itself; what a site's player buffers ahead is its choice (YouTube buffers a few minutes ahead while playing).

## 5. Proof and limits
- web-tests/seekbar.test.js (20 tests, headless Chromium, real touch taps): page ignores the tap -> jump to the tapped place (70 %, ends, red dot, playing keeps playing, paused stays paused); page seeks by itself on pointerdown / pointerup / mouseup / click after 250 ms -> no second seek; hidden controls; volume / speed / ad / lesson / far / decorative bars; live; ad; aria-valuemax; a button over the bar; the Settings switch off (asked once, again after a pause); long press and vertical swipe; slide (ignored by the page -> seek at the lift; scrubbed by the page -> left alone); Video.js, Plyr, JW Player, a custom bar; right-to-left; two quick taps; a frame of another site; cost (zero hit tests without a video); privacy of the report; the real 450 ms clock.
- core SeekStatsTest, OwnerRulesTest pin.
- NOT run: a real YouTube page (unavailable), the owner's tablet.
