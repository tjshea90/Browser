# YS1: a tap on a video player's bar must jump the video (owner message 42, 2026-11-15)
ASCII only. Newest section last. Written the moment the facts existed.

## 1. The owner's words and what they mean
"I'm on YouTube and the videos have a red seek bar. The red line is the portion of the video already watched. The red dot is the current part ... The rest of the line is a light color to the right side of the red dot for unwatched video. When I press anywhere on this bar on a regular browser or in YouTube app, it will jump the video to the place I pressed on the bar ... But in Kestrel, it doesn't jump the video at all, it just makes a strange light colored line up to the point I pressed. I want the seek bar for videos inside the browser to act like other browsers and video player apps -- if I press a spot on the video player bar, it should jump the video to that part of the bar. It should start buffering video from that point forward and not buffer anything or waste any internet on any part of the video prior to the part I am currently watching."
- "a strange light colored line up to the point I pressed" = the player's HOVER line (YouTube's `.ytp-hover-progress`, a light line from the start of the bar to the pointer). So the page DID see the touch as a pointer over the bar, and did not jump.
- Two asks: (1) the tap must jump; (2) after the jump the data must be fetched from the new place on, nothing before it.

## 2. What could and could not be found (honest)
- LATER THE SAME DAY (independent review, see section 7): the live YouTube EMBED page can be loaded in a test Chromium through the sandbox proxy (inside an iframe of another page, one tap to bring the controls up; the watch page itself is bot-checked), which gave the REAL markup of YouTube's current player bar: a custom element `yt-progress-bar.ytPlayerProgressBarHost` holding `div.ytPlayerProgressBarDragContainer role=slider aria-valuemax=100` (504x44 on a 564 px page), and inside it a 3 px rail made of four divs: `ytProgressBarLineProgressBarBackground` (opacity .3), `...Loaded` (.6), `...Hovered` (.7) and `...Played` (red), plus `yt-progress-bar-playhead` with the red dot. So the owner's "strange light colored line up to the point I pressed" is YouTube's own `Hovered` line: the page DID see the finger over the bar, painted its hover line and did not seek. The name "DragContainer" and the 2020 reports (9to5google: YouTube mobile removed tap-to-seek, a tap now only shows the position, you have to drag) say this player seeks on a DRAG, not on a plain tap. That is the most likely reason: not a Kestrel bug, a YouTube design that other apps/browsers get around or have a different variant of. The fallback below is therefore the right fix whatever the page does.
- The sandbox cannot load YouTube's watch page or embed (the agent proxy gets the bot-check page, `solveSimpleChallenge`; a Firefox user agent on Chromium is challenged too; the emulator's HTTPS to real sites rendered blank). tools/sitelab/yt_seek.js exists for the day a real page can be had.
  So the cause on the owner's tablet is NOT known. YouTube's bar code was not read either (not available).
- Ruled out by reading our own code: the only places that write a video's `currentTime` are the ad skippers (players.js:166, youtube.js:108), and only while an ad class is present; nothing reverts a seek; no script stops, cancels or replays touch / pointer / mouse events on a player (only `contextmenu` on videos in content.js and anchors in guard-main.js). The engine prefs (gecko-prefs.txt) hold no touch / pointer / mouse / hover prefs. KestrelGeckoView forwards every touch.
- Not ruled out: Gecko's fingerprinting protection (`setFingerprintingProtection(true)` in GeckoEngine.kt) changes what a page sees of a touch (pointer type, id) - see section 6 for what the emulator showed; YouTube's own mobile UI may need `touchstart` / `touchend` of a kind Gecko delivers differently; a slow press (over 300 ms) is not a "tap" to the engine's gesture detector.

## 3. Decision: a safety net that works whatever the cause (seekbar.js)
A content script (isolated world, every http(s) page and frame, document start, passive listeners only) that watches taps and slides on a player's bar and moves the video ITSELF when the page did not:
1. At `pointerdown` (only when the page has a `<video>`; otherwise nothing happens at all): the stack of elements under the finger (`elementsFromPoint`, 14 deep) is searched for a bar: a range input, `role=slider`, or a name with progress / seek / scrub / timeline / slider / playhead; rejected when the name says volume, speed, quality, caption, ad, lesson ...; shape: at least 100 CSS px wide, at most 72 high, at least 5 times wider than high; the WIDEST such element wins (YouTube nests several). It must lie over or right beside (32 px) a `<video>` of at least 120x68 and be at least half as wide as it; it and its parents must be visible (opacity product at least 0.5: the first tap on hidden controls only brings them up); no button / link / field may lie over it at the finger.
2. At `pointerup` it is a TAP (under 900 ms, under 14 px) or a SLIDE along the bar (mostly horizontal, under 8 s). A long press or a vertical swipe is not ours (the mini panel's long press stays intact).
3. 450 ms later: if any media element over that picture fired `seeking` since the finger went down (our own seeks are not counted), the PAGE did it: nothing more is done (counted as "page jumped by itself"). Otherwise the app is asked (`guard` answer carries `seek`; flag 16; the Settings row "Tap the video bar to jump", default on, nothing is done unless it is exactly true) and `video.currentTime` is set to (finger x - bar left) / bar width * length (mirrored for a right-to-left bar), clamped to `seekable`.
4. Left alone, with the reason in the report: live stream (length Infinity), length unknown / not ready, an ad plays (`ad-showing`, `ad-interrupting`, `jw-flag-ads`, `vjs-ad-playing`), the bar's own `aria-valuemax` is neither the film's length (seconds or milliseconds) nor 1 / 100, the Settings switch is off, an element vanished.
- Never: preventDefault, stopPropagation, synthesized events, `click()`, `fastSeek`, `play()` (a paused video stays paused, a playing one keeps playing). Pinned by OwnerRulesTest.
- Cost: one `getElementsByTagName('video')` per touch on a page without video; one hit test on a page with one. The report (k:'sbk': t taps, s page jumped, f Kestrel jumped, o left alone, r reason; never an address) goes out at most every 3 s and only when something was counted; the app drops private tabs.
- What it can NOT do: a player that draws its bar on a canvas or inside a shadow root (open or closed: `getElementsByTagName` and `elementsFromPoint` do not look inside); a bar with no name and no slider role; a bar that is part of a native `<video controls>` (the engine's own bar works by itself).

## 4. Buffering "from that point forward"
Not our code: after `currentTime` changes, the engine / the page's player asks for what it needs.
- A progressive file (MP4 / WebM / WAV on a plain server): the engine's media cache turns the new time into a byte offset and sends a NEW range request starting there; the part before it is not read again. `gecko-prefs.txt` limits the read-ahead (`media.cache_readahead_limit` 60 s, resume threshold 30 s, cache 64 MB) so that at most about a minute past the playhead is fetched. The lab page /seekbar-lab proves it with a real 300 s file and logs every Range header (section 6).
- A streaming player (YouTube, HLS, DASH through MSE): the page's own player asks for the segments of the new place (YouTube does not fetch what lies before the playhead after a jump); it keeps a short back buffer of what is already downloaded.
- So nothing of "waste" is added by the jump itself; what a site's player buffers ahead is its choice (YouTube buffers a few minutes ahead while playing).

## 5. Proof and limits
- web-tests/seekbar.test.js (20 tests, headless Chromium, real touch taps): page ignores the tap -> jump to the tapped place (70 %, ends, red dot, playing keeps playing, paused stays paused); page seeks by itself on pointerdown / pointerup / mouseup / click after 250 ms -> no second seek; hidden controls; volume / speed / ad / lesson / far / decorative bars; live; ad; aria-valuemax; a button over the bar; the Settings switch off (asked once, again after a pause); long press and vertical swipe; slide (ignored by the page -> seek at the lift; scrubbed by the page -> left alone); Video.js, Plyr, JW Player, a custom bar; right-to-left; two quick taps; a frame of another site; cost (zero hit tests without a video); privacy of the report; the real 450 ms clock.
- core SeekStatsTest, OwnerRulesTest pin.
- NOT run: a real YouTube page (unavailable), the owner's tablet.

## 6. Lab on the emulator (Gecko 160, the real extension, real touches), 2026-11-15, alpha47 debug build
Page: tools/sitelab/guardlab.py /seekbar-lab (bar A: the page IGNORES a tap; bar B: the page jumps itself; C: a volume slider) over a real 300 s film, /seek-big.wav = 16-bit stereo 48 kHz silence, 57.6 MB, served with byte ranges, each request logged with its Range header; the page logs every pointer / mouse / touch event, every seeking / seeked and what the engine holds (`video.buffered`). Taps are `adb shell input tap` through the app (tools/emu/glide_emu.py tap). The emulator runs without acceleration: its timings (seconds between events) mean nothing; the ORDER and the numbers do.
- What a page sees of ONE tap in Kestrel G (fingerprinting protection on): pointerdown (pointerType touch, isTrusted) -> touchstart -> pointerup (touch) -> touchend -> mousedown -> mouseup -> click (pointerType touch). `ontouchstart` is true, `maxTouchPoints` 5, PointerEvent exists. So fingerprinting protection does NOT disguise the touch; a page gets the full normal sequence. This does not explain the owner's YouTube result (YouTube was not testable); it rules one suspect out.
- Bar A (page ignores the tap): a tap at 70 percent -> `seeking A to 209.8` (a second run: 210.8) -> `seeked`; Health line `seekbar report taps=1 page=0 ours=1 left=0` (the two numbers come in two reports when the app's answer is slow, they add up).
- Bar B (page seeks itself on pointerup): a tap at 30 percent -> `page-seek B to 89.9`, exactly ONE `seeking B to 89.9`, Health `taps=1 page=1 ours=0`: Kestrel did not seek a second time.
- Buffering after the jump (the owner's second ask): before the tap the engine held `0-35` seconds of A (the whole film is 300 s). After the jump to 209.8 s the server log shows ONE new request, `GET /seek-big.wav Range=bytes=40239104-` (= second 209.6 of this 192 KB/s film: the new place), and the page's `video.buffered` went to `0-35 210-222 ... 210-242`: the engine fetches forward from the new place, keeps what it already had, and asks for nothing in between or before. The page's own jump on B did the same (`Range=bytes=17235968-`, `0-33 90-125`): a jump, whoever makes it, costs only data from the new place on.
- After the independent review (section 7), on the FINAL script, Gecko, the lab's player R = the real YouTube bar markup (yt-progress-bar, role=slider drag container, 3 px rail at opacity .3, Hovered line; the page paints only the light line): a tap EXACTLY on the 3 px rail at 70 percent (events pointerdown / pointerup touch at x=238 y=203 CSS, inside the rail's y 201-204) -> `seeking R to 210.5` -> `seeked`; Health `seekbar report taps=1 page=0 ours=1 left=0 unknown=0`. The first version rejected exactly this tap (Chromium test seekbar-real #1 fails on it). On the way: with the app asked only after the 450 ms wait and "late" set to 4 s, the same tap was left alone as `late` on the overloaded emulator (the app's first answer took over 4 s) -> the app is now asked at the touch (the answer is there when the wait is over) and late is 6 s.
- The unknown-strip path end to end: a tap on a thin wide strip under bar A with a class the script does not know -> no seek, Health `seekbar report taps=0 ... unknown=1  div.mystery-strip` (that is the line the owner's Diagnostics would carry for a bar the script does not know).
- The engine adjusts the MOUSE events of a tap to the nearest clickable element ("fluffing"): a tap 6 CSS px below bar A reached the page as pointerdown at y=234 but mousedown / click at y=228 on the bar. The script judges the stack at the pointerdown point, so a finger just outside the hit area is not a bar tap for Kestrel while the page may still get a click.
- Not shown / not trusted: the server's "all N bytes sent" lines (the adb reverse relay drains what the engine does not read, so the server cannot see what the engine consumed; use the Range header and `video.buffered`); the volume slider C was tapped once but the page has no listener on it, so only the absence of a seek was seen (the Chromium test covers it properly); nothing on a real YouTube page; no timing of any kind.
- Setup notes for the next run: a second `am start` of the same URL opens ANOTHER tab (after 11 tabs two streaming players each made the emulator crawl: `pm clear` and a single start fixed it); after `pm clear` the first page loads before the extension is ready: reload it (the toolbar's reload button, screen px 563,831) before tapping; the page area starts at screen y 26 px, scale 1.0714: a bar at CSS (x, y) is at screen px (x*1.0714, 26 + y*1.0714); a screenshot taken while two films stream is often a white or noisy frame, retry.

## 7. Independent review of seekbar.js (2026-11-15) and what was done
A fresh reviewer (a separate agent, scratch scripts in the session scratchpad, not kept; the repo keeps the regression tests) tried to break the first version with real Playwright touch taps and reported only what it reproduced. Findings, all fixed, each with a test in web-tests/seekbar-real.test.js that FAILS on the first version (9 of its 10 do):
1. (the owner's own case) YouTube's real bar was rejected when the finger lay on the 3 px rail or on the red dot: five elements are 504 px wide there, the first in the stack was the rail's background div (opacity .3), and `shown()` multiplied that opacity (0.3 < 0.5) -> "hidden". Fix: a `role=slider` / range input beats a decorative or wider element of the same width (rank), the bar's OWN opacity is its look (YouTube's rail .3, Shaka's real range input 0), hidden controls are found at a parent. Only taps off the rail (inside the 44 px slider) had worked: about one tap in ten would have done nothing.
2. Non-interactive things over the bar counted as the bar (a cookie banner, a modal scrim, a playlist of links). Fix: anything above the bar in the stack that is a link / button / field (even inside the strip, unless it is a slider thumb) or lies outside the player (the lowest ancestor of the bar that also holds the film) stops it.
3. A rating range input (`max=5`), a `<progress>`, a price slider made the video jump. Fix: `<progress>` is never a bar; an input's `max` is checked like `aria-valuemax`; a bar whose range cannot be checked must lie OVER the picture (not just beside it).
4. A live stream with a huge finite length (Shaka sets 2^32 s) and a DVR window were moved. Fix: length over 1e7 is live; `seekable` shorter than 90 percent of the length is left alone ("range").
5. Another media element's loop hid the page's own seek (one slot kept the LAST seeking event of the page; the page's seek of the film was overwritten by a looping clip's, so the script overrode the page: 5 of 5 runs). Fix: a short history of page seeks; a seek of THIS film since the finger went down is what counts. Also: our own `seeking` event is ignored for 1 s (it can come late on a busy tablet).
6. Real players missed: Plyr (the bar is 40 percent of the picture), media-chrome `<media-time-range>` (no class), Shaka (invisible input), a bar with `aria-valuemax` 1000 / 10000, Video.js' wider wrapper (a 2 percent error). Fixes: bars over the lower half of the picture may be down to 30 percent wide, custom element names count, per-mille and basis-point ranges are accepted, the bar belongs to the films of the nearest ancestor that holds any (two players on a page), rank picks the slider rather than the wrapper.
7. An answer of the app that comes 4 s after the finger went up jumped 4.5 s after the tap. Fix: over 4 s after the finger went up it is "late" and nothing is done.
NOT taken: a gate that skips the hit test unless the touched element or its parents carry a bar name (it cost the "name the unknown strip" diagnostics; the reviewer's 15 ms was measured with layout made dirty at the touch, and in the engine the layout is flushed to find the touch target before any listener runs, ours is first).
The header comment says "numbers only"; the new `n` field carries a short tag.class name (letters, digits and - _ . only, cut again by the extension's relay).
