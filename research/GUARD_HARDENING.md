# Guard hardening: tap evidence and the frame assist (RX3-G2, RX3-G3)

Rows: RX3-G2, RX3-G3 (research/REVIEW-EXTERNAL-3.md part G); G4-G6 will add their sections here. Newest first. ASCII only.

## 1. G2 - the owner's tap must not be forgeable by the page
Before: the page-world guard reported every real tap with a DOM event (`__kst_pop_v1`, detail `tap|<link>`), content.js forwarded ANY such event, the app stored `lastTapAt/lastTapHref` and let a jump through when the tap was fresh and named that very address. A page script can dispatch the same event: `tap|<its ad address>` followed by `location = <its ad address>` was ALLOWED.
Now (alpha41, `content.js` + `guard-main.js`; the app is unchanged, the same message arrives):
- content.js (the isolated world) records the owner's taps itself: a trusted `click`, or a trusted primary-button `pointerup` within 500 ms and 12 px of its `pointerdown` (a page that cancels the click still gets the tap counted). A long press, a right click, a drag and a swipe are not taps. The same link twice within 700 ms is one tap. Untrusted (script) events never count.
- What the page-world guard may report through the DOM event is whitelisted: `open`, `click`, `overlay`, `form`, `redirect` (it refused something). `tap` and `vmenu` arriving through it are dropped (`vmenu` is sent directly by content.js).
- `guard-main.js` no longer reports taps at all (it keeps its own local record for `window.open`).
- Proof: `web-tests/tap.test.js` (14 tests: forged events, untrusted clicks, touch, cancelled click, swipe, long press, right click, drag, frames, hostile details) and the changed test in `guard.test.js`. The end-to-end lab "fake the tap, then jump: STOP" belongs with G5/G6 (the app-level wall and the late-jump rule).

## 2. G3 - no channel from a page into the frames it embeds
Before: every frame's guard obeyed `postMessage('__kst_assist_v1|x|y')` from `window.parent` and then played its video or dispatched pointer events at (x, y) inside the frame: ANY page could click buttons, same-site links and script-handled elements inside ANY cross-origin frame it embeds, with no action of the owner.
Now:
- The parent's guard says "go" with a parameterless DOM event (`__kst_assist_v1`) and posts nothing.
- The parent's content.js decides from ITS OWN trusted press record (G2): the press must be at most 2 s old, at most five go-signals per press, and the frame (the `<iframe>` under the press, found with `elementsFromPoint`) and the place inside it are worked out from that press, never from the event. It sends `{k:'assist', x, y, s: iframe.src}` to the background page.
- background.js relays to the ONE child frame of the sender (by its address in `frameDoc`, or its only child; two identical embeds: no guess) with `tabs.sendMessage(tabId, {k:'assist', x, y}, {frameId})`: a channel a web page cannot use.
- The frame's own content.js plays its biggest video that has a source (muted if sound is refused) or, when the player has no video yet, presses through at that place with pointer events. It never presses on a link or a form control (A, BUTTON, INPUT, SELECT, TEXTAREA, LABEL, OPTION, SUMMARY, anything inside a FORM, editable content). Same-frame help stays in guard-main.js unchanged.
- Residual risk, said plainly: a malicious page can still put a transparent layer over a frame so that the owner's own tap lands on the layer, and then fake a refusal; the assist would replay that one tap into the frame (non-control elements only). It needs a real tap of the owner on that spot, within 2 s, at most five times per press. Before this change it needed nothing.
- Proof: `web-tests/assist.test.js` (9 tests: the old tag posted into a frame does nothing, a forged go without a press sends nothing, a stale press sends nothing, the five-per-press cap and the place coming from the press, the legitimate player-in-frame cases from AP1 ported, never on a button or link, guard off, only the extension channel), `guard.test.js` (the guard says "go" and posts nothing), `background.test.js` (4 relay tests). Emulator proof of `tabs.sendMessage` in GeckoView: see section 3.

## 3. Emulator check of the relay in GeckoView 160 (2026-11-14; Android 10 x86_64 emulator, debug build; NOT the tablet)
Lab: `tools/sitelab/guardlab.py` pages `/assist-parent` (a cross-origin frame, the same server under the host name 127.0.0.1, holding a real tiny silent media element, with a transparent layer over it that opens an ad on a tap) and `/assist-child`. A REAL tap (`adb shell input tap`) on the layer:
- the layer's handler ran and called `window.open` to the other host: refused, no request reached the server, the blocked-pop-up chip was shown;
- the extension's state line (Diagnostics, and Health `shields ext-state`) read `assist 1/1`, then `assist 2/2`: the parent's content.js asked, the background found the child frame in its table and `browser.tabs.sendMessage(tabId, msg, {frameId})` RESOLVED: **GeckoView 160 supports it**;
- the frame logged `child play event: the video STARTED`: the frame's own content.js started the video with the engine's native `play()`.
Two lab lessons worth keeping: (1) the ad address of the lab layer must be cross-site, else the guard rightly allows it after a real tap (same-site windows are allowed); (2) a content script's `video.play()` goes through the engine's own wrappers, so a page script that stubs `HTMLMediaElement.prototype.play` cannot see it (the unit tests share one world, so they can): observe the media element's `play` event instead.
Not run: a real movie site with a player frame on the tablet.

## RX3-G6 + RX3-G5 - the app's own walls use the evidence too (alpha43, 2026-11-14)
### G6 - a scripted jump to a listed ad host, at any delay
`JumpGuard.decide(..., adHost)` (core/guard): a jump to another site whose host the block lists name as an ad or tracker host is stopped at ANY delay, unless a recent real tap (within 2.5 s) was on a link to exactly this address; with a touch just now and no tap report yet it WAITS (at most 150 ms, like before). The app asks `BrowserActivity.listedAdHost(pageHost, host)`: `fs.check("https://<host>/", pageHost, RequestType.SCRIPT)` - host rules only, one hash lookup, no address or query kept. Same-site jumps and server redirects are never judged; hosts that no list names (identity providers, bing.com, facebook.com, t.co - probed with the real bundled list) are not touched. A bare `doubleclick.net` is NOT a listed host (its rules sit on subdomains); popads.net, propellerads.com, exoclick.com, juicyads.com, trafficjunky.net, clickadu.com, adsterra.com, hilltopads.net, adnxs.com, criteo.net, taboola.com are.
Proof: JVM `JumpGuardTest` +5 (long after the last touch = STOP, tap on a link to exactly that address = ALLOW, another link / no link = STOP, stale tap = STOP, touch now without report = WAIT, same site = ALLOW). Emulator (Android 10 x86_64, GeckoView 160, NOT the tablet): lab `/slowjump` (no touch for hours, 5 s after load the page sets `location` to https://propellerads.com/): Health `guard jump-judged to=propellerads.com listed=true touchAge=13525513 ms -> STOP`, `guard app-wall stopped a jump to propellerads.com`, `guard chip-show redirect propellerads.com`; the page stayed. (The first run used doubleclick.net, which is not a listed host: `listed=false -> ALLOW`: the lab target was wrong, not the rule.)
### G5 - the new-window wall needs tap evidence
`core/guard/PopupGuard.decide(sameSite, touchAgeMs, tapArrived, tapAgeMs, tapHref, uri, authHost, guardReady)` mirrors `allowOpen` of guard-main.js: no touch within 2.5 s = STOP; the same site = ALLOW; a sign-in / payment host (`PopupGuard.AUTH_SOURCE`, a copy of guard-main.js `AUTH`: `PopupGuardTest` READS that file and fails if the two differ) = ALLOW; guard not ready = STOP for other sites; tap report not arrived = WAIT (<= 150 ms, then STOP: no evidence); a tap on a link to exactly this address = ALLOW; else STOP + the blocked-window icon. `BrowserActivity.judgePopup` replaces `refusePopup` (a `$popup` rule of the lists still stops it first), `TabDelegates.onLoadRequest` / `waitForPopupTap` apply it; Health `guard app-wall stopped a new window to <host>`.
Before: ANY window that reached the engine within 2.5 s of ANY touch (the play press) was allowed. Changed behaviour the owner approved ("yes to all four"): a window opened by a BUTTON (no link) to another site that is not a sign-in host now stops and needs one tap on the icon (e.g. a Share button to a messaging site).
Proof: JVM `PopupGuardTest` (9, incl. the AUTH mirror and look-alike hosts). Emulator lab `/layer-link` (a transparent layer clicks a hidden target=_blank link to another site after a real tap): refused, `guard chip-show click 127.0.0.1` - by the PAGE guard first (kind "click"), so the app wall's new rule could not be seen working there; it is the second wall for what the page guard misses and is proven by the JVM tests only. `guardReady` is passed as true (the guard is declared in the manifest since RX3-G1; the app has no per-page signal to ask).

## RX3-G4 - a fake play layer is switched off BEFORE the press (alpha44, 2026-11-15)
Problem: the assist (AP1) is refusal-driven. The FIRST press on a player went to the layer an ad network had put over it, the ad was refused, and only the second press played ("plays on the second try").
`guard-main.js` (page world): `touchstart` and `pointerdown` (capture, passive, trusted events only) look at what lies over the player under the finger (`elementsFromPoint`, `playerAt`) and switch a FAKE layer off with `pointer-events:none !important` while the finger is still down: the click that follows is hit-tested again and reaches the player. A fake layer: positioned (fixed / absolute), no words (<= 24 characters, and the box must be empty of text to count as invisible), no picture / icon / control / link inside, holds no media, NOT inside a container that calls itself a player (jwplayer, video-js, plyr ...: its own controls and posters stay), and invisible (opacity < 0.05, hidden, or a background that is transparent or has alpha < 0.05 with no background image). Inside the video's plain wrapper (no named player around it) a layer stays untouched until this document has refused an ad (evidence that layers there are hostile): the one same-wrapper case that cannot be told from a page's own click layer.
Frames: a touch that lands inside a frame never reaches this document, so an INVISIBLE frame of ANOTHER site laid over a player is switched off by a look at the page instead (at DOMContentLoaded, at load and 1.5, 4 and 9 s later, for up to 12 player candidates).
Changed tests: the AP1 / RX3-G3 assist tests (`guard.test.js`, `assist.test.js`) used an invisible layer to provoke the refusal the assist reacts to; with G4 there is nothing left to refuse there, so their layer is now a faintly TINTED one (alpha 0.3: visible, so G4 leaves it) and the assist path stays covered. A real adversary can dodge G4 with a visible tint; the assist remains the fallback.
Proof: `web-tests/fakelayer.test.js` (9, real touch taps in headless Chromium): a fixed invisible layer: ONE tap reaches the video, no ad window; the same page WITHOUT the guard shows the layer takes the tap and opens the ad (the setup is sound); an invisible cross-site frame; a frame added 600 ms after load (found by the 1.5 s look); the player's own layer in a plain wrapper is untouched before any refusal and switched off after one; negatives: a banner with words, an opaque modal, a named player's own overlay, a layer with an icon, a tap beside the player; guard off for the site; events the page made up itself. Emulator (Android 10 x86_64, GeckoView 160, NOT the tablet): lab `/fake-layer` (an invisible fixed layer over a video): ONE real tap, the page logged `video-click` and NO `layer-click`; Health shows no refused window.
NOT done: a real movie site with a real fake layer (none could be had); the tablet.

## G4 version 2 (2026-11-15, alpha45): the guess is gone
An independent review of alpha44 (research/REVIEW_ALPHA44.md) reproduced that switching an invisible layer of the page's own document off at every touch on a player disables a page's own controls (toggle overlays, dialog backdrops, icon boxes,
touch pads) for good. Now only an invisible frame of ANOTHER site that covers at least 60 percent of a player is switched off (found by looks at 0 / 1.5 / 4 / 9 s, DOMContentLoaded and load) and it is given back when it turns visible; a layer of the
page's own document is left to the assist (evidence: a press that made the page open an ad). The older overlay-link rule was narrowed in the same round (an empty positioned link must be for another site or lie over a player; a big cross-site link must have no words).
